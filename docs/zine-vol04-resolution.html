§1 · Section I

You Aren't Where You Want to Be Because You Aren't the Person Who Would Be There 你不在你想要的位置上，是因为你不是那个会在那里的人

If you're anything like me, you think new years resolutions are stupid. 如果你像我一样，你会觉得新年决心很愚蠢。

Because most people go about changing their lives in the completely wrong way. They create these resolutions because everyone else does — we create a superficial meaning out of status games — but they don't meet the requirements for true change, which goes a lot deeper than convincing yourself you're going to be more disciplined or productive this year. 因为大多数人以完全错误的方式去改变他们的生活。他们制定这些决心是因为其他人都在做——我们在地位游戏中创造出表面的意义——但这些决心并不符合真正改变的要求，而真正改变要深得多，远不止说服自己今年会更自律或更有效率。

However, as much as I think new years resolutions are stupid, it's always wise to reflect on the life you hate so you can launch yourself toward something that much better. So whether you want to start the business, transform your body, or take the risk toward a more meaningful life without quitting after 2 weeks, I want to share 7 ideas you probably haven't heard before on behavior change, psychology, and productivity so you can do just that in 2026. 然而，尽管我认为新年决心很愚蠢，但反思你讨厌的生活总是明智的，这样你就能朝着更好的事物前进。所以，无论您是想创业、改变身材，还是冒险追求更有意义的生活而不在两周后就放弃，我想分享一些您可能从未听说过的关于行为改变、心理学和生产力方面的七个想法，以便您能在 2026 年实现这一点。

Two Requirements for Success 成功的两个要求

When it comes to setting big goals, people tend to focus on one of the two requirements for success: 在设定大目标时，人们往往只关注成功所需的两个要求中的一个：

1. Changing your actions to make progress toward the goal (least important, second order). 2. Changing who you are so that your behavior naturally follows (most important, first order). 1. 调整你的行为以朝着目标前进（最不重要，第二顺序）。2. 改变你自己，让你的行为自然而然地随之而来（最重要，第一顺序）。

Most people set a surface-level goal, hype themselves up to remain disciplined for the first few weeks, then go back to their old ways without much struggle, because they were trying to build a great life on a rotting foundation. 大多数人设定一个表面目标，自我激励保持纪律几周，然后不费吹灰之力又回到老样子，因为他们试图在腐朽的基础上建造一个伟大的生活。

Think of Somebody Successful 想想一个成功的人

Think of a bodybuilder with a great physique, a founder/CEO worth hundreds of millions, or a charismatic dude who can chat up a group without a shred of anxiety. Do you think the bodybuilder has to "grind" to eat healthy? Does the CEO have to discipline themselves to show up and lead the team? 想想一个身材完美的健美运动员，一个身家数亿的首席执行官，或者一个能轻松与一群人交谈而毫无焦虑的迷人家伙。你认为健美运动员必须"努力"才能吃得健康吗？CEO 必须自律才能出现在团队面前并领导团队吗？

To you, it may seem like that on the surface, but the truth is that they can't see themselves living any other way. The bodybuilder has to grind to eat unhealthily. The CEO has to force themself to lie in bed past their alarm clock, and they hate every second of it. 对你来说，表面上看可能是这样，但事实是，他们无法想象自己能以其他方式生活。健美运动员必须努力才能吃得不健康。CEO 必须强迫自己在闹钟响过之后躺在床上，并且他们讨厌每一秒钟。

If you want a specific outcome in life, you must have the lifestyle that creates that outcome long before you reach it. If someone says they want to lose 30 pounds, I often don't believe them — not because I don't think they are capable, but because there are too many times when that same person says, "I can't wait until I'm done losing weight so I can start to enjoy life again." 如果你想在生活中获得特定的结果，你必须在你达到它之前，就拥有能创造这种结果的生活方式。如果有人说他想减重 30 磅，我常常不相信他们——不是因为我认为他们没有能力，而是因为那个人经常会说，"我迫不及待想结束减肥，好重新开始享受生活。"

When you truly change yourself, all of your habits that don't move the needle toward your goal become disgusting, because you have a deep and profound awareness of what kind of life those actions compound into. 当你真正改变自己时，所有那些不能推动你实现目标的习惯都会变得令人厌恶，因为你深刻地意识到这些行为会积累成怎样的人生。

Core Idea 核心观点

All behavior is goal-oriented. Your "bad" habits serve an unconscious goal. 所有行为都是目标导向的。你的"坏"习惯服务于一个无意识的目标。

§2 · Section II

You Aren't Where You Want to Be Because You Don't Want to Be There 你不在你想要的位置上，是因为你不想待在那里

If you want to change who you are, you must understand how the mind works so that you can start to reprogram it. The first step is to understand that all behavior is goal-oriented. It's teleological. 如果你想要改变你自己，你必须了解心智是如何运作的，这样你才能开始重新编程它。第一步是理解所有行为都是目标导向的。这是目的论的。

You take a step forward because you want to reach a certain location. You scratch your nose because you want to make the itch go away. Those ones are clear, but most of the time, your goals are unconscious. You may not realize that when you sit on the couch in the middle of the day, you are trying to burn time before your next responsibility. 你向前迈步是因为你想到达某个地方。你挠鼻子是因为你想让痒的感觉消失。这些例子很清晰，但大多数时候，你的目标是潜意识的。你可能没有意识到，当你中午坐在沙发上时，你正试图在下一个责任开始前消磨时间。

Procrastination as Goal-Seeking 拖延作为目标追求

If you can't stop procrastinating your work, you may justify it with the fact that you "lack discipline," but in reality, you are attempting to achieve a goal like you always are. In this case, that goal could be to protect yourself from the judgment that comes from finishing and sharing your work. 如果你无法停止拖延工作，你可能会用"缺乏纪律"来为自己辩解，但实际上，你仍然像往常一样试图实现一个目标。在这种情况下，那个目标可能是为了保护自己免受完成和分享工作的评判。

If you say you want to quit your dead-end job, but stay in it without any real reason, you might think you don't have enough courage — but the truth is that you are pursuing the goal of safety, predictability, and an excuse to not look like a failure. 如果你说你想要辞掉死路一条的工作，但没有任何真正的原因还留在那里，你可能会开始觉得自己不够勇敢——但事实是你在追求安全、可预测性，以及一个借口，以免在他人面前显得像个失败者。

Real change requires changing your goals. Not setting surface-level goals — changing your point of view. A goal is a projection into the future that acts as a lens of perception. 真正的改变需要改变你的目标。不是设定表面目标——而是改变你的观点。目标是未来的一种投射，它充当一种感知的镜头，让你能够注意到有助于你实现目标的信息、想法和资源。

§3 · Section III

You Aren't Where You Want to Be Because You're Afraid to Be There 你不在你想要的位置上，是因为你害怕到达那里

Here's how you've become who you are today, and how you will become who you will be tomorrow. This is the anatomy of identity: 这是你如何成为今天的你，以及你将如何成为明天的你的方式。这是身份的解剖：

1. You want to achieve a goal → 2. You perceive reality through the lens of that goal → 3. You only notice "important" information that helps → 4. You act and receive feedback → 5. You repeat until it becomes automatic → 6. That behavior becomes who you think you are → 7. You defend your identity to maintain psychological consistency 1. 你想实现一个目标 → 2. 你通过这个目标的镜头感知现实 → 3. 你只注意到能帮助你的"重要"信息 → 4. 你行动并获得反馈 → 5. 你不断重复直到它变得自动化 → 6. 那种行为成为你认为自己是谁的一部分 → 7. 你为了保持心理一致性而捍卫你的身份

The unfortunate reality is that you must break the cycle between steps 6 and 7, but this process starts when you are a child. You have the goal of survival. You are dependent on your parents to teach you how to survive. You had to conform. And since the way most people teach is through reward and punishment, unless you adopt their beliefs and values, you will be punished. 不幸的现实是，你必须打破步骤 6 和 7 之间的循环，但这个过程从你小时候就开始了。你的目标是生存。你依赖父母教你如何生存。你必须顺从。由于大多数人通过奖惩来教你，除非你接受他们的信念和价值观，否则你会受到惩罚。

Conceptual Survival 概念层面的生存

Once you fulfill your physical survival needs (which is quite easy in today's world), you start to survive on the conceptual or ideological level. You may not try to protect and reproduce your body, but you absolutely protect and reproduce your mind. 一旦你满足了身体的生存需求（这在当今世界很容易做到），你开始在概念或意识形态层面生存。你可能不会试图保护和繁殖你的身体，但你绝对会保护和繁殖你的心灵。

When your body feels threatened, you go into fight or flight. When your identity feels threatened, the same thing happens. If you are heavily identified with a political ideology, you will feel threatened when someone challenges your beliefs. You literally feel the stress. You feel, emotionally, like you were just slapped in the face. 当你的身体感到威胁时，你会进入战斗或逃跑模式。当你的身份感到威胁时，同样会发生这种情况。如果你与某种政治意识形态有强烈的认同，当有人挑战你的信念时，你会感到威胁。你真的会感到压力。在情感上，你感觉就像被人当面扇了一巴掌。

The same thing happens when you unconsciously see yourself as a lawyer, a gamer, or somebody else who would not take the actions to achieve a better life. Your identity is running the show, and it will fight to stay alive. 当你无意识地认为自己是一个律师、一个游戏玩家，或是一个不会采取行动来获得更好生活的人时，同样的事情也会发生。你的身份在掌控全局，而且它会为了存活而战斗。

§4 · Section IV

The Life You Want Lies Within a Specific Level of Mind 你想要的生活存在于特定的意识层面

The mind evolves through predictable stages over time. When you're born, you're like a little survival sponge that absorbs whatever beliefs you can so that you can feel safe and secure. And if you don't be careful, your mind may crystalize and it may make it difficult to live a meaningful life. 意识会随着时间的推移经历可预测的阶段。当你出生时，你就像一块小小的生存海绵，吸收你能吸收的任何信念，以便你感到安全。如果你不小心，你的意识可能会结晶化，这可能会使你难以过上有意义的生活。

The 9 Stages of Ego Development (Abridged) 九个自我发展阶段（简版）

Impulsive — No separation between impulse and action. A toddler hits when angry because the feeling and the behavior are the same thing. 冲动型——冲动和行动之间没有区别。幼儿生气时会打人，因为感受和行为是同一回事。

Self-Protective — The world is dangerous and you learn to look out for yourself. A kid learns to hide report cards and figure out what adults want to hear. 自我保护——世界是危险的，你学会照顾自己。一个孩子学会隐藏成绩单，弄清楚成年人想听什么。

Conformist — You are your group and its rules feel like reality itself. Someone who genuinely cannot fathom why anyone would vote differently than their family. 遵从者——你属于你的群体，群体的规则感觉就像现实本身。有人真诚地无法想象为什么任何人会投票与他们的家庭不同。

Self-Aware — You notice you have an inner life that doesn't match the exterior. Sitting in church and realizing you're not sure you believe what everyone around you seems to believe. 自我认知——你注意到你有一个与外表不匹配的内心生活。坐在教堂里，意识到你不确定自己是否相信周围所有人似乎相信的东西。

Conscientious — You build your own system of principles and hold yourself accountable to them. Leaving your family's religion after careful study and adopting a personal philosophy you can defend. 认真负责——你建立自己的原则体系，并要求自己遵守它们。在仔细研究后离开家族宗教，采用自己能够辩护的个人哲学。

Individualist — You see that your principles were shaped by context and start holding them more loosely. Realizing your political views have more to do with where you grew up than objective truth. 个人主义——你意识到自己的原则受到环境的影响，开始对它们持更宽松的态度。意识到你的政治观点更多地与你的成长环境有关，而不是客观真理。

Strategist — You work with systems while aware of your own involvement in them. Leading an organization while actively questioning your own blind spots. 战略家——你在系统中工作，同时意识到自己参与其中。领导一个组织时，积极质疑自己的盲点。

Construct-Aware — You see all frameworks, including your identity, as useful fictions. Holding your spiritual beliefs metaphorically not literally, knowing the map is not the territory. 建构自觉者——你将所有框架，包括你的身份，都视为有用的虚构。以比喻而非字面的方式持有你的精神信仰，知道地图并非疆域。

Unitive — Separation between self and life dissolves. Work, rest, and play feel like the same thing. There's no one left who needs to become something, just presence responding to what arises. 合一——自我与生命的分离消融。工作、休息和玩耍感觉都一样。再也没有人需要成为什么，只有存在回应着出现的事物。

For most people reading this, I would assume you hover between stages 4 and 8, which is a huge gap. The good thing is, it doesn't really matter what stage you are in, because moving through any of them follows a pattern. 对于大多数阅读这篇文章的人来说，我猜你的得分在 4 到 8 之间，这是一个巨大的差距。好的事情是，你处于哪个阶段并不重要，因为通过任何一个阶段都遵循一个模式。

§5 · Section V

Intelligence Is the Ability to Get What You Want Out of Life 智力是你在生活中获得你想要的东西的能力

There is a formula for success. One ingredient is agency. One ingredient is opportunity. The last ingredient is intelligence. 成功有一个公式。其中一个成分是自主性。一个成分是机会。最后一个成分是智力。

If you have high agency but low opportunity, it doesn't matter how likely you are to act toward a goal, because it isn't a goal that will bear much fruit. If you have opportunity and agency but low intelligence, then you will never be fully able to benefit from that opportunity. 如果你有高自主性但机会低，那么你采取行动实现目标的可能有多大都不重要，因为那不是一个能结出丰硕果实的目标。如果你有机会和自主性但智力低，那么你将永远无法完全从那个机会中获益。

Cybernetics: The Art of Steering 控制论：掌舵的艺术

Cybernetics comes from the Greek word kybernetikos which means "to steer" or "good at steering." It's also known as "the art of getting what you want." Cybernetics illustrates the properties of intelligent systems: 控制论源自希腊语单词 kybernetikos，意为"掌舵"或"擅长掌舵"。它也被称为"获取你想要的艺术"。控制论说明了智能系统的特性：

1. Have a goal → 2. Act toward that goal → 3. Sense where you are → 4. Compare it to the goal → 5. Act again based on feedback 1. 有目标 → 2. 朝着目标行动 → 3. 感知你现在所在的位置 → 4. 将它与目标比较 → 5. 根据反馈再次行动

A ship blown off course that corrects toward its destination. A thermostat sensing a change in heat and turning on. The pancreas excreting insulin after blood glucose spikes. You can judge intelligence based on the system's ability to iterate and persist with trial and error. 一艘偏离航线的船修正方向驶向目的地。一个恒温器感知到温度变化并开启。胰腺在血糖升高后分泌胰岛素。你可以根据系统迭代和坚持试错的能力来判断其智能程度。

High intelligence is the ability to iterate, persist, and understand the big picture. The mark of low intelligence is the inability to learn from your mistakes. Low-intelligence people get stuck on problems rather than solving them. They hit a roadblock and quit. 高智能是迭代、坚持和理解大局的能力。低智能的标志是无法从错误中学习。低智力的人容易在问题上卡住而不是解决问题。他们遇到障碍就放弃。

High intelligence is realizing any problem can be solved on a large enough timescale. The reality is that you can achieve any goal you set your mind to. 高智力是认识到任何问题在足够长的时间尺度上都可以解决。现实是，你可以实现你决心要达成的任何目标。

§6 · Section VI

How to Launch Into a Completely New Life (in 1 Day) 如何在一天内开启全新的生活

The best periods of my life always came after a period of getting absolutely fed up with the lack of progress I was making. Through the simple, but often painful act of questioning, you can reach profound insights that change the trajectory of your life. 我人生中最美好的时期，总是出现在我对自己的进步毫无进展感到极度厌烦之后。通过简单但常常痛苦的行为——质疑，你可以达到深刻的见解，从而改变你的人生轨迹。

This protocol is structured so that it can be completed in one day. In the morning, you do a psychological excavation. During the day, you prompt yourself with interrupts. At night, you synthesize the insights into a direction. 该协议设计为可在一日内完成。早上，你进行心理挖掘，揭示自己隐藏的动机。白天，你通过中断提示自己，避免自动驾驶，并思考你的生活。晚上，你整合见解，形成你明天将开始行动的方向。

Part 1 · Morning — Psychological Excavation 第一部分 · 早晨——心理挖掘

Set aside 15-30 minutes. Do not outsource this contemplation to AI. Break past the limiter that is on your mind. 预留 15-30 分钟。不要试图将这种思考外包给 AI。我想让你打破脑海中那个限制。

1. What is the dull and persistent dissatisfaction you've learned to live with? (If you don't hate it, you will tolerate it) 1. 你学会了忍受的那种迟钝而持久的不满是什么？（如果你不讨厌它，你就会容忍它）

2. What do you complain about repeatedly but never actually change? Write down the three complaints you've voiced most often in the past year. For each: What would someone who watched your behavior (not your words) conclude that you actually want? 2. 你反复抱怨却从未真正改变的是什么？写下过去一年你最常表达的三个抱怨。对于每个：一个观察你行为（而不是你的话）的人会得出你实际上想要什么结论？

3. What truth about your current life would be unbearable to admit to someone you deeply respect? 3. 关于你目前生活的真相，是什么会让你无法向一个你非常尊敬的人承认？

Then, build your anti-vision: If absolutely nothing changes for the next five years, describe an average Tuesday. Where do you wake up? What does your body feel like? What's the first thing you think about? Now do it for ten years. What have you missed? Who gave up on you? 然后，构建你的反愿景：如果未来五年内绝对没有任何改变，描述一个普通的周二。你在哪里醒来？你的身体感觉如何？你首先想到什么？现在用十年时间来做。你错过了什么？谁放弃了你？

Part 2 · Throughout the Day — Interrupting Autopilot 第二部分 · 全天——打断自动驾驶

Create reminders in your phone at random times. Include the question so you can immediately start thinking about it: 在手机上创建随机时间的提醒。在提醒中包含这个问题，这样你就可以立即开始思考它：

11:00am — What am I avoiding right now by doing what I'm doing?
1:30pm — If someone filmed the last two hours, what would they conclude I want from my life?
3:15pm — Am I moving toward the life I hate or the life I want?
5:00pm — What's the most important thing I'm pretending isn't important?
7:30pm — What did I do today out of identity protection rather than genuine desire?
9:00pm — When did I feel most alive today? When did I feel most dead? 上午 11:00——我正在做的事情是什么让我现在在回避？
下午 1:30——如果有人拍摄了过去的两个小时，他们会得出我想要怎样的生活结论？
下午 3:15——我是在走向我讨厌的生活还是我想要的生活？
下午 5:00——我假装不重要的事情中，什么是最重要的？
下午 7:30——我今天做了哪些事情是出于身份保护而非真诚的愿望？
下午 9:00——我今天什么时候感觉最活着？什么时候感觉最死？

Part 3 · Evening — Synthesizing Insight 第三部分 · 晚上——综合洞察

Write a single sentence that captures what you refuse to let your life become. Write a single sentence that captures what you're building toward. Then set your goal-lenses: 写下一句能概括你拒绝让人生变成的样子的话。写下一句能概括你正在努力的方向的话。然后设定你的目标镜头：

One-year lens: What would have to be true in one year for you to know you've broken the old pattern? One-month lens: What would have to be true in one month for the one-year lens to remain possible? Daily lens: What are 2-3 actions you can timeblock tomorrow that the person you're becoming would simply do? 一年镜头：一年后，你需要知道你已经打破了旧模式，必须发生什么具体的事情？一个月镜头：一个月后，什么情况必须发生，才能让一年镜头的可能仍然存在？每日镜头：明天，你能安排 2-3 个行动，成为你想成为的人会自然而然做的事情是什么？

Core Idea 核心观点

Games are the poster child for obsession, enjoyment, and flow. Reverse-engineer them to live in deeper enjoyment. 游戏是痴迷、享受和心流状态的典范。反向工程它们以活在更深层次的享受中。

§7 · Section VII

Turn Your Life Into a Video Game 将你的生活变成一个视频游戏

You now have all of the components that lead to a good life. Organize all of your insights into one coherent plan. Write down these 6 components: 你现在拥有了所有导致美好生活的组成部分。整理你所有的见解到一个连贯的计划中。写下这 6 个组成部分：

1. Anti-vision — What is the life I never want to experience again?
2. Vision — What is the ideal life I think I want and can improve as I work toward it?
3. 1-year goal — What will my life look like in 1 year time?
4. 1-month project — What do I need to learn? What can I build?
5. Daily levers — What priority tasks bring my project closer to completion?
6. Constraints — What am I not willing to sacrifice? 1. 反愿景——我再也不想体验的生活是什么？
2. 愿景——我认为想要并可以为之努力改善的理想生活是什么？
3. 一年目标——一年后我的生活将是什么样子？
4. 一个月项目——我需要学习什么？我能构建什么？
5. 每日杠杆——哪些优先级高、推动指针的任务能让我离项目完成更近？
6. 约束条件——我不愿意为了实现愿景而牺牲什么？

Why is this so powerful? Because these components literally create your own little world. You turn your life into a video game: 为什么这如此强大？因为这几个要素实际上为你创造了自己的小世界。你把生活变成了一场电子游戏：

Your vision is how you win. Your anti-vision is what's at stake. Your 1-year goal is the mission — your sole priority in life. Your 1-month project is the boss fight where you gain XP. Your daily levers are the quests. Your constraints are the rules that encourage creativity. 你的愿景是你获胜的方式。你的反愿景是赌注所在。你的一年目标是任务——你生活中唯一的优先事项。你的一个月项目是 Boss 战，你如何获得经验值。你的每日杠杆是任务。你的约束是那些鼓励创造力的规则。

All of these act as a concentric set of circles, like a forcefield, that guard your mind from distractions and shiny objects. The more you play the game, the stronger this force becomes, and soon enough it becomes who you are — and you wouldn't have it any other way. 所有这些构成一个同心圆环系统，像一道力场，保护你的心灵免受干扰和闪亮物体的侵害。你玩得越多，这种力量就越强大，很快它就成了你的本质——而你也不会希望有其他的样子。

The Protocol 协议
Summary 总结

True change isn't about discipline or surface-level goals. It's about becoming the kind of person whose behavior naturally produces the outcome you want. 真正的改变不在于纪律或表面目标。而在于成为那种行为自然而然产生你想要的结果的人。
